PLM vs Excel: when a spreadsheet stops being enough for a fashion brand

Excel works until it doesn't. Seven signs your fashion tracker has outgrown a spreadsheet, and what to try before you buy a PLM.

Srishti Dhawan, founder of Spindle
18 Oct 2026

Excel is a perfectly good way to run production when you've only got a handful of styles. It stops being good when the spreadsheet is out of date the moment you save it. If that sounds familiar, you don't automatically need a PLM, but you do need to change something. Here's how to tell, and what your options are.

Why Excel wins in the early days

It's free, you already know it, and your suppliers send you Excel anyway. Costing sheets, order forms, packing lists: they all arrive as spreadsheets. When you're running one season with a couple of factories, a single tracker and a shared folder is honestly fine. Don't let anyone tell you otherwise.

What a PLM gives you that Excel can't

PLM stands for product lifecycle management. It's software that keeps each style's details, files and history in one system. In practice that means:

  • Controlled versions, so a factory isn't working from last month's tech pack
  • Files attached to the style instead of scattered across inboxes and drives
  • Permissions, so the right people see the right things (and sometimes suppliers can log in)
  • A record of who changed what, and when

That's real value. The catch is that a PLM only knows what someone types into it. If the information arrives by email, and for most fashion teams it does, somebody still has to retype it. A PLM also asks the whole team to change how they work, which is why plenty of PLM projects end with people quietly going back to Excel.

For a fuller explainer, see What is PLM for fashion?

The middle option: keep Excel and email, stop retyping

There's a third route that gets overlooked. Leave your tools alone, and add something that reads your supplier emails for you.

That's what we're building at Spindle. You forward supplier emails to a Spindle address. It works out which style each email is about, pulls out details like stage, cost, quantity and delivery date, and proposes them to you. You glance, tap "Looks right" or fix it, and the style card keeps every email and file for that style in order. It won't always get things right, which is why nothing counts until you've looked at it.

It isn't a full PLM. It doesn't create your tech packs or build your costings. It's for teams whose real information lives in email and who are tired of retyping it.

So which one?

  • Stay with Excel, plus the habits above, if you've got a few styles and one or two people.
  • Look at a PLM if you've got a bigger team, lots of factories, and people who'll enter data properly every day.
  • Try an email-reading tool if your main problem is retyping and finding what was agreed.

Whichever you pick, run it on one season first. Don't move everything on day one.
