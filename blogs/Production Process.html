The fashion production process, step by step: the document behind every step

A step-by-step map of the fashion production process, from tech pack to shipment, showing the documents, formats and handoffs at every stage.

Srishti Dhawan, founder of Spindle
19 Oct 2026

A dress takes months to make, and most of the time what goes wrong is not the dress. It is the paperwork around it.

The wrong trim colour gets ordered because an approval was in an email nobody forwarded. Someone prices from last season's cost sheet. A date changes on a call and never gets back to the calendar. The garment is fine. The information around it has slipped.

With more than 20 years of experience in the fashion industry, both as part of a manufacturing business and in retail in the UK, I have mapped out these processes across retailers. There are small changes here and there, but the essence is the same. This post covers the eight stages a style goes through, the documents that go with each one, the format they come in, and where things get lost.

This is a general map, so use it to find your own gaps, not as a rulebook. Companies do some things differently, like how many sampling rounds they run or who signs off colour, but the shape is the same almost everywhere.

Audit your own process in 20 minutes

Pick one live style, ideally one that is mid-season. Get the people who work on it into one room, or onto one call, and go through the eight stages. Don't prepare. What you answer from memory is the honest answer. For each stage, answer three questions:

1Where is the latest version of this stage's document?
2Who last changed it?
3Could a colleague find it in two minutes without asking you?

What to do with this map

Run the audit above on one live style first. The stages where the answer is "in my inbox" are your gaps.

Most teams find the same two or three. Approvals scattered across emails. A date that changed in one place and not another. A cost nobody can trace back to where it was agreed. Fix those first with what you already have: one folder per style, one line per decision, one place for the current dates.

FAQ

Is this the same process for every brand?
The stages are close to universal. The detail changes. A brand with simple styles may skip a sampling round. A brand with its own factory has fewer handoffs. Use the map as a checklist, not a standard.
Do I need software to run this process?
Not at the start. Plenty of small brands run it on email and Excel for a long time. The question is whether the gaps above are costing you time or mistakes. If they are, it is worth looking at your options.
