Asana, Trello and Notion can run a fashion production calendar. Here's what they do well, where they fall short, and how to set them up.

Short answer: yes, for tasks and deadlines. Not so much for the details of each style. If you're a small brand and you're not ready for a PLM, a project tool is a sensible way to run your calendar. Just know where it'll let you down.

What these tools do well

Tasks, owners, due dates, reminders and boards. Your team probably knows one of them already, the plans are free or cheap, and you can start today. For "who's doing what by when", they're hard to beat.

Which tool?

I'm not going to crown a winner, because it depends how your team works. Roughly: board tools in the Trello style are the quickest to learn. Notion's databases are the most flexible if you want custom fields and different views. Asana is strong on tasks, deadlines and team workloads. Features and pricing change, so try a free plan on one real season before you commit.

The email gap

Project tools manage the tasks you create. They don't read the supplier email that changes the plan. So your board is only as good as your typing, and the typing is the part that slips.

Where Spindle fits

It closes the email gap. Forward supplier emails to Spindle and it matches them to styles, picks up stages, quantities and delivery dates, and proposes them for you to check. Every email and file for a style stays together on one card.

Once dates are shared on email, Spindle tracks them for you. The Production Board shows every style and its delivery date in one view, so it becomes your tracker without anyone retyping a date. Keep your project tool for team tasks if you like it.
