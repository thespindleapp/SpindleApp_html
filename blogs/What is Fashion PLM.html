The short version
PLMproduct lifecycle management

PLM stands for product lifecycle management. In fashion, it's software that keeps everything about a style in one place, from the first sketch to the shipped order, so your team and your suppliers work from the same information. That's the short version. Here's the longer, more honest one.

Who uses it

Designers and technical designers use it for tech packs and specs. Merchandisers and production managers use it to track progress and deadlines. Buyers and sourcing teams use it for costs and suppliers. Sometimes the suppliers get a login too. At a small brand, it's often the same two people wearing all of those hats.

Where it goes wrong

Here's the part sales decks skip

A PLM only knows what somebody types into it. In fashion, most real updates arrive by email and WhatsApp. If nobody has time to move those into the system, it falls behind, people stop trusting it, and they go back to the spreadsheet. Plenty of brands pay for a PLM and end up using it for tech packs only.

A PLM also asks everyone, you included, to work its way. Setting it up takes real effort, and so does keeping the data clean.

PLM vs ERP

People mix these up.

ERPERP software runs the business side: orders, stock, invoicing and finance.
PLMPLM covers product development: design, specs, materials, samples and approvals.

Many brands use both and connect them.

Where Spindle fits

Spindle isn't a full PLM. It doesn't create tech packs or run costings. It does the part many PLMs struggle with, which is getting information out of supplier emails. You forward emails to a Spindle address. It matches each one to a style, proposes details such as stage, cost, quantity and dates, and you check them. Everything for a style sits on one card, and a board shows your whole season from the values you've checked.

Some brands may use it on its own, others alongside a PLM. It won't always get a value right, which is why you check each one. A Gmail and Outlook plugin is planned for later.
