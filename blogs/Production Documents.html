The documents of fashion production: a field guide

A field guide to the documents in fashion production: tech pack, cost sheet, PO, lab dip, fit comments, T&A and more, and what usually goes wrong with each.

Srishti Dhawan, founder of Spindle
19 Oct 2026

I have looked inside a lot of production folders. Most of them look the same. A tech pack in one place, a cost sheet in another, a PO nobody is sure is the latest, and the real answer sitting in an email from three weeks ago.

This guide covers the nine documents that carry a style from brief to shipment. For each one: what is in it, who usually owns it, what format it arrives in, what should be on it, how it goes wrong and what helps. It is written to be passed around. Send it to a new merchandiser, your buyer, your finance team or your supplier.

If you are new, read it top to bottom. If you have been in production for years, go straight to the checks between documents and the audit at the end. That is where most of the time is saved. The guide follows the order a style moves through the process.

When the document isn't a document

A lot of production runs on things that are not documents. A photo on WhatsApp. A screenshot of a spreadsheet. A voice note. A PDF of an Excel sheet. You will not stop it, and it is often faster. But a decision made this way only counts once it is written down somewhere a colleague can find it.

Audit your document set in 20 minutes

Pick one live style, ideally mid-season. Get the people who work on it on one call and go through the nine documents. Don't prepare. What you can find from memory is the honest answer. For each document, answer these four questions:

How to read it. Every "no" is a gap. Start with the documents closest to the PO, because mistakes there are the most expensive. Then look for a pattern. If "where is it?" keeps getting the answer "in my inbox", you have more of an email problem than a document problem.

Early warning signs

The documents rarely tell you a style is in trouble. The way people talk about them does. These are the signs I listen for. If you hear two on the same style, stop and check the documents against each other.
