A simple system for tracking supplier emails, sample approvals and agreed changes in fashion production, using only email, folders and Excel.

Srishti Dhawan, founder of Spindle

In fashion production, your real records aren't in a system. They're in your inbox. The lab dip approval from three weeks ago, the quantity change, the new ex-factory date someone mentioned on line nine of a reply. This post is a practical way to keep hold of all that with the tools you already have, and what to do when it gets too big for one person.

Naming attachments

Use STYLE_item_version_date, for example SS27-0142_costing_v2_2026-03-12.xlsx. Save files into the style folder as soon as they arrive. Don't leave them in your downloads folder. We've all done it.

Where this starts to creak

The system works until volume goes up. Sixty styles, three factories, two people, and the end-of-day sweep slips. Then you're back to retyping from your inbox into a tracker, and you're only as current as your last sweep.

Letting software read the emails

A word on privacy

Supplier emails contain prices, order volumes and people's names. Before you forward them to any tool, ours included, ask where the data is stored, who can see it, and whether it fits what you've promised your suppliers. In the UK, check how personal data such as names and email addresses is handled. Any vendor worth using will answer that plainly.
